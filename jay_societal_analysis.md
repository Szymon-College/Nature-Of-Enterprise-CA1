# Consumer behaviour

- The transformation of streaming services highlights the behavioural shift for consumers. In the early 2000s, consumers would purchase these films which were distributed on DVDs, VHS cassette tapes and CDs which also applies for music. These were mainly available for purchase from stores such as Xtra-vision, HMV or Golden Disc. However, consumers would gradually shift to cloud streaming platforms. According to Whitten (2019), the purchases of DVDs declined in 2008 by "more than 86 percent" and platforms such as Netflix or HBO had a sale increase of "1231% to $12.9 billion" in the US market. The consumers would watch films provided and distributed from its database servers.

- Music streaming platforms became increasingly popular and it also contributed to the decline of physical music consumption. Spotify was launched in 2008 as a form to prevent piracy and became a cloud music streaming platform after 2 years. Bilgili Sülük (2026) highlights that consumers were drawn to features of Spotify such as "on-demand playback" and "offline listening". Spotify achieved "10 million" users in 2010 which shows the platform caters on user experience and data personalization. Rolling Stone (2018) highlights the plunging sales of CDs to "80 percent" over the previous decade, from "450 million to 89 million" and it demonstrates that consumers were moving away from physical formats.

- As these streaming platforms increasingly became widespread, transactional purchases declined in film and music specialist retailer. From a social analysis perspective, this would represent the change of ownership-based consumption to access-based consumption. 

# Mental wellbeing, screen time and personal habits

- The popular demands of Netflix, Amazon Prime, Hulu and other streaming platforms made an impact on younger generations' mental wellbeing, additionally it contributes bad personal habits and high volume of screen time. Venkatesh, Sripriya and Rajkamal (2026) highlights younger generations, primarily Gen Z where these individuals would "multitask" and "binge-watch" leading to "cognitive overload" that could affect their "learning" and "academic performance". It also explains why high frequency use of these platforms does not result in better overall knowledge.
