### Introduction
This case study examines the change in how people consume media and changing their habits and how businesses deliver and produce media through the transformation of streaming services. Streaming any form of media from a server to a user's device requires the internet. The data of the media gets sent to a user's device by a server through the internet where the user can consume their media without the need of downloading the media file (Pollete, 2024). Streaming is often associated with videos, but it can be done with any form of media such as music and games as well.

This sudden shift of media consumption does not just affect consumers and businesses that provide streaming services, but other related businesses such as the extinct businesses that rented media to consumers are also affected. The world has been becoming more interconnected as ever before and this includes all things streaming related. People can watch movies and shows produced from one country despite living in a different country where they do not speak the language the media was produced in (Gul, 2023). One widely known example of this was Squid Game which was a show that was produced in South Korea and gained popularity in 2021 (About Netflix, 2025).

This topic will be looked at through two different viewpoints, societal and business. People are willing to trade ownership of their media for comfort and convenience for an ongoing fee. With the newer generation having better access and understanding of streaming, they are beginning to form unhealthy consumption habits around streaming services. Some businesses have died and some have flourished as streaming media has become more mainstream. Video rental businesses such as Blockbuster filed for bankruptcy in 2010 (Bitter and Olito, 2023) whilst newer streaming services such as Disney+ prospered as they gained a steady stream of income in a highly scalable environment. These perspectives will analyse how the media and related industries and the everyday lives of people have been affected on a global scale.

### Historical development

1877-78-Phonograph The first case of physical media was the invented phonograph in 1877, invented by Thomas Edison. The device was a cylinder wrapped in tinfoil to record and reproduce sound.

1889-Vinyl A disc-shape design , it used a needle liked the phonograph which would moved on the grove of it's surface to reproduce the sound. The vinyl Became the main use to record and listen to sound during this era.

1963-Cassette Cassette are magnetic analog tapes , this made physical media more consumer friendly as they were more sturdy and less bulky than Vinyl. Cassettes fit easily into pockets, bags and car stereos in which Vinyl could not , With Vinyls being a fixed location

1970-90 The first cases of physical media for film were the VHS and Betamax , these formats used magnetic tape to record and show film.

1979-Walkman Sony brought out the Walkman which used cassettes to play sounds in a small device, this was a stand out for physical media in music as it helped bring Cassettes to more homes across the world and outside the home with it's portability

1982-present-CDs Sony and Philips brought out a the compact disc (CD), It's main purpose was to unify digital audio and film formats. It read music and film using a laser instead of a needle or tape , it brought much better audio quality than previous formats and proper switching, rewind and sound skipping without gradual sound degradation associated with repeated cassette playback.

Other physical formats followed with future versions (MiniDisc , DVD etc) sharing the same design with each version improving sound quality , higher definition and larger storage capacity

1998-Digital formats This marked the major turning point to physical to the modern day streaming. First in 1998 a new format called MPEG Audio Layer III (MP3) which compressed music files, this meant that audio files could be played and stored on computers and the possibility of transforming the file over the internet , this marked the beginning of physical to digital music.

1999–2003 -Music File Sharing and online store Napster was launched as an online music-sharing service, allowing users to share and download MP3 files directly from one another. this marked the first threat for physical media as Napster was a major success with it being a Illegal way to download music over the internet .

Apple's iTunes Music Store in 2003 came out which was a legal digital store backed by music labels, this made music labels happy as they had a way to earn money over the internet but threatened physical media with it being more convenient than physical.

Streaming 2007- Netflix introduced it's online streaming service in 2007, allowing users to watch films and television programmes over the internet . This was the end for Physical media films as after 2008 major stores like blockbuster and Extra-vision closed in the next years after Netflix launched it's online streaming.

In 2008 , Spotify launched with it being that users could access a large online music library and play tracks whenever they wanted on the internet anytime.

Streaming has since become a major source of income for the recorded music and film industry, while sales of physical music formats have declined. However, physical formats such as vinyl, CDs and DVDs continue to be sold and collected, meaning that physical media has not completely disappeared.

### Societal Analysis
#### Consumer behaviour
Before the era of cloud streaming platforms, the option to buying films and music were through stores such as Xtra-vision, HMV and Golden Discs where they sold DVDs, VHS tapes and CDs. As streaming platforms had became known, there was less of a need for consumers to go to these stores as films and music could now be accessed online. It made an impact on DVD sales and it began to plummet as data shows it dropped by "86 percent" and platforms like Netflix and HBO showed a sale increase, "1231 percent", which is "$12.9 billion" in the US market (Whitten, 2019).

Music was subjected similarly as films as Spotify was introduced in 2008 to prevent music piracy as it holds a large collection of music and it had "on-demand playback" and "offline listening" features which made it more consumer friendly. In 2010, there were "10 million" users (Kuyucu, 2026, p. 322) so CD sales dropped by "80 percent" from "450 million to 89 million" (Knopper, 2018) which shows more consumers opted in for music streaming instead of buying CDs.

It shows that consumers are willingly paying monthly subscriptions to gain instant media content which shows there would be no need to buy individual films or albums and with devices such as smartphones, tablets and smartwatches it made listening to music and watching films on the go much easier.

#### Mental wellbeing in young people
From the 2000s and the previous decade, young people would watch DVDs and also television shows where they had to wait to come on and as Netflix, Amazon Prime or other streaming platforms became known, young people began to indulge watching shows whenever they wanted. This brought issues for the younger generations as they have the opportunity to watch the whole series in one sitting. Venkatesh, Sripriya and Rajkamal (2026) explains the younger generations would indulge in "binge-watch" and with this, it resulted to "cognitive overload" which led them to poor "learning" and "academic" standards (Venkatesh, Sripriya and Rajkamal, 2026). It shows that "62 percent" prefers to be using their smartphones, tablets and TVs than sleeping and it highlights that "30 percent" resulted in poor sleeping patterns as they continue to indulge late night shows (Tucker, 2025).

As entertainment continues to be easily accessible and readily available from smart devices, young people would willingly spend more time staying up late watching shows and it would mean less hours of sleep which it could affect how well they do in education.

#### Encourage cultural diversity
In the booming age of physical media, it was difficult to come across films and shows from other countries and as streaming platforms such as Netflix brought international entertainment to a wider audience, it would recommend shows to viewers. This gave users more of a chance to come across shows from different countries as Netflix had a large collection of "international content" in which Guha (2023, p. 5) states it is "a bridge" between communities.

One of the features that stood out from Netflix was the option to change the language of a show or series and it allowed watching international shows much easier as viewers were not required to learn and understand the language of the country. An example would be a Danish series, The Rain "85 percent" where American Netflix viewers preferred watching it with English dubbing and the German series, Dark were "78 percent" (Salsabila, 2021, p. 20).

The rise of international shows saw more people taking an interest in other cultures with one of the biggest cases being the Korean series Squid Game where people in New York began playing traditional Korean games from the show. This shows that streaming platforms brought a wider audience and also gave people the chance to learn and take part in the culture of other countries.

### Business Analysis
Streaming video subscription and video advertising revenues have raced higher as a result rising from just over 2 billion $ in combined revenues into 2010 to likey surpassing the 100 billion $ mark by 2026. while some of streaming's headlong growth has come at the expense of traditional multichannel,TV network and film renvenue streams,impacts have varied widely.Media giants including Disney comcast/NBCU, paramount and fox Corp.Have also leaned heavily into the streaming model, aiming to offset declining traditional TV revenues with digital dollars.

The media industry has changed dramatically.First transitioning from analogue to digital, then SD to HD and UHD also from SDI to IP.Add the shift consumer habits to these technological milestones and you have an almost unrecognisable entity compared to the one that existed only 15 to 20 years ago.

The digital economy is a sector in its own right which exists alongside others such as manufacturing and retail (Jordan,2020:p1-17).Some firms are identified as operating within it, such as software developers and video game publishers. Most businesses and sectors, however are not born digital but have embarked on a process of digitization.it is the case of media and entertainment, and the platformization of televison is among the most visible signs of this process. streaming platforms can be defined as those whose business model rests on video monetization.They overlap but are distinct from social media platforms that stream videos but whose business case does not entirely rest on them (Cunningham and Craig,2019).The former includes Facebook watch, Neflix and Youtube, instagram and twitter.

While VOD pre-existed streaming and was developed by cable and sateilite pay-TV services (Rookie,2009):p216-234;(Tydeman and kelm,1986);(Vittet-philippe,1999), platforms have brought changes to the monetization formats.Advertising has always been a key revenue stream for broadcasters,but it is streaming that brought AVOD to the forefront of the TV industry in the 2000s.

Streaming platforms innovate on two other counts. First, they are scaling up these monetization models. Pay-Tv services operated on a national or multinational basis. The world's largest, sky, has developed a presence in a handful of European territories and claims around 17 million customers. Broadcasting was a national industry that progressively internationalized, streaming is a global business that is progressively localizing. No platform is born global and even Netflix waited several years before crossing borders(Randolph,2019).

### Ethical Reflection
The growth of streaming services such Netflix, Spotify and digital gaming platforms has completely changed how people access entertainment.
These days people can watch movies, download video games and listen to music without the need to visit a physical store.
Although streaming is convenient and gives customers more choice, it has also created some ethical problems.
These include the loss of jobs, digital ownership(which means people no longer own the content that they have paid for), privacy and the way some artists are paid for their work.

#### Job Losses and the Decline of Physical Stores  
Traditional shops such as Xtra-Vision and Blockbuster were once the most popular places to go whenever you wanted to rent a movie  to watch with your family or even rent a 
video game, however they have struggled the most  as most customers moved toward the digital entertainment route due to convenience and lower upfront cost and then adding a low monthly payment without having to leave their house.
In January 2016, Extra-Vision entered liquidation, this has put approximately 583 jobs at risk(O'Faolain, 2016).
This also happened to Blockbuster as it also faced serious financial difficulties, although they had problems with increasing competition and a large amount of debt rather than streaming alone(Blockbuster Inc.,2011)
This has also happened to major Gaming retailers such as GameStop as they have had to compete with digital game distribution while still actively selling physical copies(GameStop Corp.,2025)

#### Digital Ownership and Consumer Rights 
Another concern is that customers no longer physically own much of the content that they have paid to access.
For example, purchasing a DVD means that the customer only pays once and kept it for life, could lend it to a family member or friend and watch it without a subscription.
However, Netflix subscribers can only receive access to content under the service's terms which means that they have to pay in order to access.
Netflix also states that that titles may become unavailable as its catalogue changes over time(Netflix,2026).
Another ethical concern is digital ownership when consumers purchase video games, for example when buying a game via the Playstation Store, they're actually buying a license in order to play the game instead of owning the game outright. According to Playstation's Software Usage Terms(Playstation, n.d.), customers are granted permission to use the software under certain conditions, but they do not own the software itself. This means that the games that we buy digitally are tied to our specific account and they cannot be sold or lended to someone else like they used to be with physical discs. The physical discs also contained licensed software but the biggest difference is that the customer still owned the content after it was purchased. This also includes loss of games and content if the account gets suspended or closed. ( Sony Interactive Entertainment,2026)
This raises questions about how much control consumers actually have over their entertainment.
Streaming companies should be able to clearly explain what their customers are paying for and including any restrictions on downloading and accessing content.
The European Commission(n,d)also outlines consumer protections for digital services, showing why clear digital rights are important.

#### Privacy and Personal Data 
Privacy is another issue that is very important.The platforms that we use to stream (Netlifx, Hulu, Disney Plus) can use our information about what we like to watch and listen in order to provide a personsalised recommendation based on our watched history.According to Ireland's Data Protection Commission(n.d.), under GDPR laws they must be clear and upfront about how personal information is used and stored.
These watch recommendations can be useful, this shows how much personal information these companies can collect from their customers.Most users might not immediately realise how much of their information is stored and/or used.
This is a huge privacy concern especially since most people agree to their terms and conditions without reading them properly and failing to fully understand them. Organisations must be clear  and upfront about how personal data is used under the GDPR laws. The streaming services that we use should be able to make privacy settings easier to understand and should give customers greater control over their watch history and especially their personal information.

#### Fair Payment for Artists and Creators 
Since streaming music now is more popular than ever, thanks to streaming services like Spotify, Apple Music and Sound Cloud etc, it has made it easier for artists to reach a larger audience of listeners worldwide, but this has some concerns over how the streaming revenues are distributed.
According to a study from the UK's Competition and Markets Authority (2022), the top 0.4% of artists were responsible for more than 60% of music streams in 2020. This is an example of smaller artists can struggle to attract the same number of listeners.
The report also showed an estimate that an artist could potentially earn around £12,000 from 12 million UK streams annually, but less than 1% have managed to achieve that amount of streams.
Artists also don't receive all the money they make from their streams as most of it goes to the record label and others that own the rights to their music.

#### Overall Reflection and Improvements 
Streaming has brought a lot of benefits, convenience and a huge selection of entertainment options that also makes content easier to access, however with the decline of physical media, these changes have affected workers, consumers and creators. 
In my opinion streaming itself is not  unethical, but large companies such as Netflix, Disney Plus, Spotify and Apple Music should take way more responsibility for the consequences of technological change. 
They could provide better support for  employees, protect customer data, improve digital consumer rights and make artist payments more transparent.

### Conclusion
The transformation of streaming services was a sudden and unexpected change in the digital era which has forever changed how people produce, consume and sell media. Through the societal viewpoint, we can see that people prefer having constant access to a massive library of all entertainment for a monthly fee rather than owning a limited library of their favourite media. Consumers are able to explore shows that were made internationally but they may be subjected to an algorithm that constatntly recommends them shows which can alter their sleeping habits. If consumers only stuck to hardware, it would be harder for them to explore new media but they would not be subjected to a streaming provider using their data and watch history to keep them on their platforms. Through the business viewpoint, we can see that streaming is extremely profitable for companies who have leaned into the digital era and adapted to the new digitalization of products. Despite similar concepts already existing before, streaming has brought a new light to playing pre-recorded media and profiting off of it, gaining an increasing customer base every year. Despite streaming platforms flourishing, older businesses that didn't adapt to the change became irrelevant as streaming became the main source of entertainment.

The rise of streaming services brought a lot of changes, one of the most important aspects are consumer rights and their privacy. People who pay for streaming services put themselves into some risk by giving the business who provides the streaming service their personal data and are forced to trust them they will not use them for any malicious intent. Despite streaming services being sudden, the business should show some responsibility such as transparent use of consumer's data, providing affordable and accessible services for everyone and paying the producers and other jobs involved in creating the media. It is important that when massive changes in an industry like this happens, we must accommodate for everyone.
