# Ethical Reflection and Improvements 
**The growth of streaming services such Netflix, Spotify and digital gaming platforms has completely changed how people access entertainment.**
**Although streaming is convenient and gives customers more choice, it has also created some ethical problems.**
**These include the loss of jobs, digital ownership(which means people no longer own the content that they have paid for), privacy and the way some artists are paid.**

## Job Losses and the Decline of Physical Stores  
**Traditional shops such as Xtra-Vision and Blockbuster struggled the most as customers moved toward the digital entertainment route.**
**In January 2016, Extra-Vision entered liquidation while also putting approximately 583 jobs at risk(O'Faolain, 2016).**
**This also happened to Blockbuster as it also faced serious financial difficulties, although they had problems with increasing competition and a large amount of debt rather than streaming alone(Blockbuster Inc.,2011)**
**This has also happened to major Gaming retailers as they have had to compete with digital game distribution while still actively selling physical copies(GameStop Corp.,2025)**

# Digital Ownership and Consumer Rights #
**Another concern is that customers no longer physically own much of the content that they paid to access.**
**For example, purchasing a DVD means that the customer only pays once and kept it for life, could lend it to a family member or friend and watch it without a subscription.**
**However, Netflix subscribers can only receive access to content under the service's terms which means that they have to pay in order to access.**
**Netflix also states that that titles may become unavailable as its catalogue changes over time(Netflix,2026).**
**This raises questions about how much control consumers actually have over their entertainment.**
**Streaming companies should be able to clearly explain what their customers are paying for and including any restrictions on downloading and accessing content.**
**The European Commission(n,d)also outlines consumer protections for digital services, showing why clear digital rights are important.**

# Digital Exclusion #
**Streaming also creates difficulties for people that don't have a reliable internet connection and according to Central Statistics Office(2025),**
approximately 95% of Irish households had internet access in 2025 which means that a very small proportion of households had no internet access.**
**Because most physical rental shops have now become less common, people who cannot access streaming services may have less entertainment options,
especially the elderly or those who are unfamiliar with digital technology and may find the transition quite challenging.**
**Some improvements that could be done are to offer more affordable services, have support for offline downloads and preserve backup access routes to
access movies, games and music.**

# Privacy and Personal Data #
**Another important issue is Privacy.The platforms that we use to stream can use our information about what we like to watch and listen in order to provide a personsalised recommendation based on on our watched history.According to Ireland's Data Protection Commission(n.d.), organisations must be clear and and upfront about how personal data is used under the GDPR laws. The streaming services that we use should be able to make privacy settings easier to understand and should give customers greater control over their watch history and especially their personal information.**


# Fair Payment for Artists and Creators #
**Since streaming music now is more popular than ever, it has made it easier for artists to reach a larger audience of listeners worldwide, but this has some concerns over how the streaming revenues are distributed. According to a study from the UK's Competition and Markets Authority(2022) it was found that more than 60% of music streams were associated by the top 0.4% of artists. This shows that based on their study that in 2020 that just 0.4% of artists accounted for more than 60% of music streams, this also shows how a very small percentage of artists attract most of the listeners, but thousands of smaller artists struggle to the same attention and recognition that they deserve. The report also showed that the potential earnings for an artist could be around £12,000 from 12 million streams annually, but less than 1% of artists managed to achieve that. Even though streaming can give independent artists the opportunity to reach a larger audience without signing with a record label, earning a decent enough income can be very challenging. A possible improvement to this problem would be for the streaming platforms to make their payment systems more transparent and allowing artists to understand how their earnings are calculated.They can also improve by offering fairer contracts and much better support for independent artists.**

# Overall Reflection and Improvements #
**Overall, streaming has brought a lot of benefits, convenience and huge selection of entertainment options that also makes content easier to access, however with the decline of physical media this has affected workers, consumers and creators. In my opinion streaming itself isn't unethical, but these big streaming companies such as Netflix, Disney Plus, Spotify and Apple Music etc should take way more responsibility for the consequences of technological change. They could start by providing better support for their employees, protecting customer data, improving digital consumer rights and making their payments for artists way more transparent. This would make the entertainment industry more fair. Finding a balance between technological progress and protecting the people affected by it is the main challenge unfortunately.**
