# Historical Development
  

# Music and Film
1877-78-Phonograph
The first case of physical media was the invented phonograph in 1877, invented by Thomas Edison. The device was a cylinder wrapped in tinfoil to record and reproduce sound.

1889-Vinyl
A disc-shape design , it used a needle liked the phonograph which would moved on the grove of it's surface to reproduce the sound. The vinyl Became the main use to record and listen to sound during this era.


1963-Cassette 
Cassette are magnetic analog tapes , this made physical media more consumer friendly as they were more sturdy and less bulky than Vinyl. Cassettes fit easily into pockets, bags and car stereos in which Vinyl could not , With Vinyls being a fixed location 


1970-90 The first cases of physical media for film were the VHS and Betamax , these formats used magnetic tape to record and show film.


1979-Walkman Sony brought out the Walkman which used cassettes to play sounds in a small device, this was a stand out for physical media in music as it helped bring Cassettes to more homes across the world and outside the home with it's portability 


1982-present-CDs 
Sony and Philips brought out a the compact disc (CD), It's main purpose was to unify digital audio and film formats. It read music and film using a laser instead of a needle or tape , it brought much better audio quality than previous formats and proper switching ,rewind and sound skipping without gradual sound degradation associated with repeated cassette playback.


Other physical formats followed with future versions (MiniDisc , DVD etc) sharing the same design with each version improving sound quality , higher definition and larger storage capacity


1998-Digital formats 
This marked the major turning point to physical to the modern day streaming. First in 1998 a new format called MPEG Audio Layer III (MP3) which compressed music files, this meant that audio files could be played and stored on computers and the possibility of transforming  the file over the internet , this marked the beginning of physical to digital music.


1999–2003 -Music File Sharing and online store
Napster was launched as an online music-sharing service, allowing users to share and download MP3 files directly from one another. this marked the first threat for physical media as Napster was a major success with it being a Illegal way to download music over the internet .


Apple's iTunes Music Store in 2003 came out which was a legal digital store backed by music labels, this made music labels happy as they had a way to earn money over the internet but threatened physical media with it being more convenient than physical.

Streaming
2007- Netflix introduced it's online streaming service in 2007, allowing users to watch films and television programmes over the internet . This was the end for Physical media films as after 2008 major stores like blockbuster and Extra-vision closed in the next years after Netflix   launched it's online streaming.

In 2008 , Spotify launched with it being that users could access a large online music library and play tracks whenever they wanted on the internet anytime.

Streaming has since become a major source of income for the recorded music and film industry, while sales of physical music formats have declined. However, physical formats such as vinyl, CDs and DVDs continue to be sold and collected, meaning that physical media has not completely disappeared.
